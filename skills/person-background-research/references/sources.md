# Where to look

Search in this order within each section. Stop once a section is well covered for this person's footprint.

## Identity and resume
- LinkedIn paste or PDF from the user (best source)
- Personal website, About page, speaker kit or bio page (often has a downloadable resume or CV)
- Company team/leadership page, faculty page, press-release boilerplate bios
- Wikipedia (public figures) — read the whole article, including "Early life" and "Personal life"
- SEC filings (proxy statements, Form 4) for public-company executives and directors — bios with exact dates
- Crunchbase-style profiles for founders and investors
- Stanford users: for licensed people databases (BoardEx, Capital IQ, PitchBook), use `gsb-library-scout`

## What each company does
- The company's own site (About, history, press)
- Wikipedia, Crunchbase, news coverage of funding rounds or acquisitions from the years they were there
- For defunct companies: archived site via the Wayback Machine, shutdown coverage

## Education and mascots
- LinkedIn, Wikipedia, alumni magazines, commencement or award announcements
- High school: local news ("local grad…"), alumni features, yearbook mentions they've posted, their own interviews ("growing up in…")
- Mascots and rivalries: the school's athletics site or Wikipedia page

## Writing
- Google Scholar, SSRN, arXiv (papers)
- Amazon / Goodreads author pages (books)
- Author pages on outlets (Muck Rack for journalists lists bylines by outlet and date)
- Substack, Medium, personal blog, company blog
- X / Bluesky / Threads / LinkedIn posts — pinned posts and long threads
- First published piece: earliest bylines on Muck Rack, student newspaper archives, college paper archives, Wayback Machine of old blogs; journalists often mention their first job in "about" pages

## Interviews
- Podcast search: Listen Notes, Apple Podcasts, Spotify, YouTube; search "[name] podcast" and "[name] interview"
- Use transcripts where available; otherwise show notes, episode descriptions, and YouTube auto-captions
- Written: Q&As, "how I work" columns, alumni profiles, local news features, company blog spotlights
- Conference talks on YouTube

## Personal life (public, self-shared only)
- Their own social posts and bios (public accounts only)
- Interview tangents ("outside of work I…")
- Race results, charity event pages, nonprofit board listings they've publicized
- Wikipedia "Personal life" for public figures

## Right now
- Google News: person (30 days), company (90 days)
- Their last 90 days of posts
- Journalists: Muck Rack and the outlet's author page for recent stories
- VCs: fund site portfolio and team page, their own posts on investment focus
- Execs: company newsroom, earnings calls (public companies), job postings
- Hiring managers: company careers page filtered to their team

## Contact
- Personal site contact page, author bios, conference speaker pages, papers (corresponding author email), press releases (media contacts)
- Email pattern: other public addresses at the same domain (press contacts, papers, public GitHub commits on company repos)
- Stated preferences: "how to pitch me" pages, pinned posts, FAQ pages
